32504 area facts

High ground, mostly 40 to 120 ftMost of ZIP 32504 sits well above the forecast 5 to 7 ft coastal surge; Pensacola International Airport, inside the ZIP, is at 121 ft. Rain runoff and wind are the risks here.
Mostly outside zones A to EMost of 32504 has no evacuation zone polygon in the Florida statewide layer; low ground along Bayou Texar and Escambia Bay does. The Escambia order covers Zones A and B (noon Thursday). Check your address at Know Your Zone.
FEMA zones vary by streetTurn on the FEMA flood zones layer on the map, or look up your address at msc.fema.gov. Street ponding is possible anywhere with 4 to 8 in of rain.
Tornado and tree riskTornado risk is highest east of the center, and the 10 AM track now puts you just west of it, but rain bands spin up tornadoes on both sides. Interior room away from windows, shoes and helmets handy, phone alerts on loud.
Open water nearbyBayou Texar on the southwest edge of 32504 and Escambia Bay on the east are surge water. Streets near them can flood; the higher middle of the ZIP does not.

Home plan if you are staying: high ground, outside the evacuation zones

Under an evacuation order? Go, do not ride it outThis plan is only for homes outside Zones A and B and off the low ground near Bayou Texar and Escambia Bay. If your address is in an ordered zone, follow the Escambia order and use the shelters listed below.
Wind comes around, so trees fall two waysThe 10 AM track puts the center about 20 mi east of you, so you are on the west side of the eye. Winds arrive from the northeast and north this afternoon, hold from the north northwest at closest approach, then swing west and southwest overnight. Trees north and northeast of a house lean toward it first; trees west of it threaten it after midnight.
Pick the room by the trees, not by the floor planFirst floor, interior, no exterior walls if possible, and not under the fall line of the tallest trees on either side. A room with the second floor above it is good: the upper floor takes the first hit from a falling trunk.
Stage the room before dark FridayMattress or cushions, shoes on, helmets or bike helmets for everyone, flashlights (no candles), water, pet crates, meds, the go bag, a charged power bank each, and the NOAA radio.
Upstairs is for daylight onlyClose every upstairs door to slow wind if a window fails. Lay towels at the base of upstairs doors. If you hear a crack or the roof breach, nobody goes up to look until the wind is done.
Water from above, not belowOn the high ground of 32504, the flood risk is a breached roof or a blown window. Keep a tarp, duct tape and contractor bags downstairs, and know where the main breaker is: if water reaches wiring, kill the power at the panel.
Tornado warning means the same room, right nowRain bands on the east side spawn quick tornadoes Friday night. Phone alerts loud, radio on, everyone already sleeping in or next to the safe room.
Cars and the garagePark away from the tallest trees. Brace the garage door from inside if you can; a lost garage door pressurizes the house and lifts the roof.
The lull is not the endWith a 25 mile wide eye tracking near you, the wind may drop to calm for 20 to 40 minutes, then return from the opposite side harder. Stay put until NWS says the center has passed.
